What is herd immunity?

Herd immunity is when enough people in a group are protected from a disease so that it struggles to spread. This protection can come from vaccines or from recovering after having the disease.

The key idea: if most people are protected, even people who can’t be vaccinated (like some babies or people with certain health conditions) are safer, because the disease can’t find new people to infect.

Two important ideas

  • R0 (say “R nought”) is how many people, on average, one sick person infects when nobody is immune.
  • Herd immunity threshold ≈ 1 − (1 ÷ R0). If R0 = 3, the threshold is about 67%.

Try these challenges

  1. Set R0 to 2 and increase vaccination. When do outbreaks stop growing?
  2. What vaccination level is needed when R0 = 4?
  3. Can a small number of infections still happen even above the threshold? Why?
Teacher notes
  • Model: simple grid with random contacts per day. Recovery gives immunity. Vaccines are modelled as 100% protection for simplicity.
  • Learning aims: read charts, understand R0, relate vaccination to spread, discuss randomness and real-world complexity.
  • Extensions: vary recovery days, add partial vaccine effectiveness, compare different R0 values.
